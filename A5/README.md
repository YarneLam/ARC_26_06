## 2. Group Reflection



### 2.1 Feedback on Our A3 Tutorial



For A3, we developed a tutorial demonstrating how Python and IfcOpenShell could be used to check door information in an IFC model.



The feedback we received was generally positive. The other students found the tutorial well structured and easy to follow, particularly the step-by-step explanations and the Excel report.



They suggested adding more screenshots and visual examples to make the tutorial easier for beginners, as well as explaining the difference between PASS, FAIL and REVIEW more clearly.



Since the submission deadline had already passed when we received the feedback, we did not revise the tutorial. However, the feedback highlighted the importance of clear explanations and visual guidance when communicating technical information.



### 2.2 Did Our A4 Tool Address the A2 Use Case?



In A2, our aim was to develop an IFC-based tool to investigate whether the corridors in Building 2608 had sufficient capacity for the expected number of occupants.



We believe that our A4 tool successfully addressed a large part of this use case. It identified corridors, measured their widths, estimated occupant loads and compared the results with selected BR18 references. The results were presented in Excel and JSON reports.



However, the tool also has limitations. Some rooms lacked sufficient information to estimate occupancy, and certain door connections and corridor measurements still require manual verification.



The project demonstrated how automated BIM analysis can support design decisions, but also how dependent the results are on the quality of the IFC data.



Therefore, we consider the tool useful for identifying potential issues rather than providing a complete fire-safety assessment.



### 2.3 Application in Advanced Building Design



We believe that the tool is most relevant during Stage C – Developed Design, when the building layout is being developed and potential corridor-capacity issues can still influence design decisions.



By identifying possible problems early, designers can review corridor dimensions and expected occupant loads before the design is finalised.



Overall, the process from A2 to A4 helped us understand how a BIM use case can be developed into a working analysis tool, while also showing the importance of reliable model information and professional judgement.
